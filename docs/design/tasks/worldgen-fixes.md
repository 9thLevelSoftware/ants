## Work
1. **Generation time.** Profile a standard world of every Tier 1 archetype (the machine is shared and loaded: take the minimum of several runs and compare shares, not absolute times). The known offender is the column rasteriser: `rasterColumns` spends most of its time in `searchColumn`, a 53-evaluation search run for every tilted box and cylinder, and paving slabs are all tilted by the design's 1:60 fall; primitives are also rasterised in three separate passes. Replace the search with an analytic column for rotated boxes and cylinders (keep the search only for shapes that have no closed form), compute each primitive's columns once and share them between the passes, and remove any other clear waste you find in the derived-map step. The maps' contents must stay the same within a stated tolerance: prove it with a test that compares old and new columns over several worlds (keep the old routine available to that test). Report before/after per step and per archetype.
2. **The cross-map walker tests** (`kernel.test.ts`, three archetypes red). The test's walker steers horizontally towards navigation-cell centres and gets stuck where a cell is open only through a gap narrower than the cell, or on small clutter. Find out which side is wrong and fix that side: if the navigation map marks cells passable that a walker of the species' size cannot pass, fix the map (clearance-aware passability); if the map is right and the test's walker is naive, make the test follow the path the way the game's ants do (the locomotion module's public path following, or a kernel walker with the same local avoidance) — without weakening what the test proves: a walker really can cross every generated map along a navigation path.
3. **The other red tests.** `maps.test.ts` (kitchen floor at +150: furniture-covered cells legitimately read higher), `maps.verify.test.ts` E5 (the door-crack gap for the larger carpenter-ant species when the door has the design's weather-strip variant: the design then forces another penetration — make the check accept the passage the design guarantees, and make sure that passage is really wide enough for that species), `pipeline.test.ts` (the "wood nesters without catalogue wood" case needs a fixture that really has no catalogue wood now that fences, sheds and door jambs carry decayed wood; the Node-budget cases must pass once step 1 is done — keep a load-tolerant bound with a strict mode behind `FOUNDRESS_PERF_STRICT=1`), `terrain.test.ts` (tree pits in the pavement are loam by design: exempt those cells). In every case keep the test's intent; say in the report what each test proves afterwards.
4. **Hand-over notes for the validation owner** (do not edit their files): the rest-on-ground check tests only the centre of the lowest primitive's bounding box and misjudges objects lying across paving joints; the cavity-proximity helper ignores terrain-hole voids, so drain grilles are marked "no gain"; the rule that only asphalt cracks with at least 300 BU of slot count toward the total is missing from the evaluator. Confirm each with a small reproduction and put the reproduction in your report.

## Acceptance criteria
1. `npm run typecheck` clean; every test file you own is green in default mode; the nine listed tests pass for the right reason (each explained in the report).
2. Generation time for a standard world, minimum of five runs, per archetype, before and after, per step; the derived-map step is at least twice as fast on woodland-edge and kitchen-door; the equivalence test for columns passes.
3. World fingerprints: state whether they changed. Derived maps may change only within the stated tolerance; placed objects, cavities and routes must not change at all (a test compares object lists before and after on three seeds per archetype — capture the "before" lists first).
4. Determinism holds (same seed, same fingerprint, across two processes).
