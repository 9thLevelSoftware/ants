# Task look-pass — Bring the world's surfaces, plants and props to the look reference (Stage 1B, Opus, visual)

## Goal
The look-development spike set the target: naturalistic macro photography at ant scale (`docs/research/spike-look.md`, images in `docs/research/spike-look-img/`, above all `high-midday.png`, `high-golden.png`, `high-overcast.png`). The running game's generated worlds do not reach it yet. Close the gap on the things the player looks at most: the ground under the ant, plants, everyday materials, props, sky and light. This is an art pass judged by screenshots, with the reference images beside them.

## Evidence of the gap (look at these first)
- `test-output/stage1a/01-surface-third-person.png`, `05-workers.png`: flat ground plane with speckle-noise gravel (no grains with volume, texture shimmering at grazing angles), plants as discs on poles, no foreground or background shapes.
- `test-output/wgo/shots/kitchen-door-dandelion.png`: grass blades are convincing; the dandelion is a flat disc on a stick. `woodland-edge-log.png` is close to the target (log, pebbly soil, leaf litter) — use it as the in-game benchmark.
- `test-output/wgo/shots/car-park-verge-parked-car.png`, `test-output/interior-fix/shots/s5-overview.png`, `s5-cabinets.png`: every metal object (car body, fridge, bin, chair legs and even a chair seat) wears the same rust-blotched scan, so a kitchen reads as a scrapyard.

## Work (in this order; stop adding and start polishing when time runs short — a few things done well beat many half-done)
1. **Audit.** Build the gallery script: for each Tier 1 archetype (one seed) take ant-height frames at `high` quality with a low macro camera (the spike's framing: about 4.5 body lengths back, pitch about 17 degrees, 50 degree field of view, look target ahead of and above the ant) at the start position, beside a plant, beside a large prop, on hard paving, and indoors where the map has an interior; plus a material swatch page (every `MaterialId` on a box, a cylinder and the ground, lit by sun and by overcast sky, with a worker ant for scale). Put each frame beside the reference and list the differences honestly, largest first. This list is your plan; include it in the report.
2. **Materials.** Every `MaterialId` must read as what it is at ant scale, in sun, shade, overcast and wet. In particular: `metal` becomes clean painted or bare metal driven by the primitive's tint (car paint with a clear-coat highlight, white enamel for appliances, galvanised grey for bins and grates, chrome where tagged); rust appears only where a template asks for it (tag) or as light edge wear; `plastic`, `glass`, `ceramic`, `fabric`, `rubber`, `paper`, `food` likewise get believable response and micro-detail. Fix texture shimmer and speckle at grazing angles (mip bias, anisotropy, dual-scale blending, detail fade with distance). Check grain scale against real sizes (a 4 mm ant on pea gravel stands among stones its own size; concrete shows aggregate and pores, not noise).
3. **Ground with volume.** Near the camera the ground needs relief the ant visibly walks among: instanced grit, pebbles and crumbs matched to the ground material (the decor layer has the machinery), relief or parallax in the terrain material where instancing is too costly, contact darkening under stones and under ants. It must stay consistent with the simulation's ground height (purely visual relief stays within the foot offset so ants neither float nor sink).
4. **Plants.** Dress the procedural plants on the render side without changing their collision primitives: leaves with a leaf silhouette, midrib and veins, slight curl, and back-lit translucency; stems with nodes and fine hairs where apt; flower heads built from petals or florets instead of a flat ellipsoid (dandelion flower and seed clock, daisy, clover head, generic umbel), buds and seed heads where the template says so. Trees, logs, stumps and bark: believable bark relief at ant scale, moss and lichen patches, cut ends with rings. Fungus caps with gills. Keep instance counts and shader cost inside the quality budgets (report the numbers).
5. **Props and interiors.** The catalogue's everyday objects must be recognisable from ant height: give templates sensible tints and material choices (car body colours from a small palette, tyres rubber, glass dark, a white fridge, a steel pedal bin, coloured toy plastics, glazed ceramic bowls), add render-only dressing where one primitive cannot carry the read (panel seams, wheel hubs, bowl rims, food surfaces such as sugar crystals and crumbs), and make sure large silhouettes stay in the background of every habitat as the spike recommends.
6. **Sky and light.** Sky gradient, sun and moon discs, haze that turns distant shapes into soft silhouettes, and the lighting rig's balance per time of day and weather must match the reference frames (clear midday, golden hour, overcast after rain, moonlit night); shadows at ant scale stable under camera motion.
7. **Near-camera occlusion fades for the world.** GDD 01 §6.5 rule 3: terrain, primitives and decor that come between the possession camera and the ant are thinned with the same ordered dither the ants and nest walls already use. The data arrives as `FadeOutput` (`src/render/camera/types.ts`: a cone from the camera to a point short of the ant, with strength). Add an optional input `occlusion?: FadeOutput | null` to `WorldFrame` (`src/render/world/lighting/types.ts`) and consume it in the terrain, primitive, decor and grass materials (the world-slot engineer passes `FrameInfo.fade` into it — that exact field name is agreed with them). Shadows are not dithered. The ground the ant stands on must never be dithered away (only what rises between camera and ant).
8. **The founding queen** is shown with wings folded over her gaster although she has already flown: unless GDD 06's opening explicitly has her shed them on screen, she must be wingless (with wing scars) from the first frame. Find where caste and wing state reach the ant renderer and fix it at the source you are allowed to touch; report if the cause is in the simulation's data.
9. **Gallery and report.** Re-run the gallery; before/after pairs for every frame, at `high`, and the same frames at `medium` and `low` to prove the look degrades gracefully; frame cost before/after from the renderer's statistics (draw calls, triangles, and timing where the shared machine allows).

## Acceptance criteria
1. `npm run typecheck` clean; tests in your paths green; no console errors in the harness; the classic build still runs the gallery.
2. The after-gallery, looked at and judged honestly beside the reference images: ground with real relief near the camera and no shimmer; plants that read as plants (leaf shapes, real flower heads); metals, plastics, glass and ceramics that read correctly, with no rust where none belongs; props recognisable; sky and haze giving depth in every archetype; the founding queen wingless. Every remaining difference from the reference is listed.
3. World generation output is unchanged except for tints and tags (fingerprint statement in the report); the simulation is untouched; ants still stand on the ground (no visible float or sink in the gallery frames).
4. Draw calls and triangles stay within the budgets in `docs/03-architecture.md` §6 for each quality preset (report before/after); runtime quality switching still works.
5. All new textures or shapes are procedural or built from assets already in the manifest (no downloads, originality rule respected).
